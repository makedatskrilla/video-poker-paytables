Jacks or Better 9/6 vs 8/5: what the paytable costs

Two Jacks or Better machines can look the same and still pay back very different amounts. This page has the exact return for the 9/6 and 8/5 paytables, how often each hand lands, a ranked hold chart and a price tag on 21 common holding mistakes. Everything is computed by a short Python script that goes through all 2,598,960 deals and all 32 ways to hold each one, and the results are checked against published tables.

The paytables

Pays are per unit bet with maximum coins. A royal flush pays 4,000 coins for 5, which is 800 per unit. The names 9/6 and 8/5 come from the full house and flush lines, the only two that differ. Everything else is the same: one 52 card deck, five cards dealt, one draw, and a pair of jacks or better is the lowest paying hand.

The pays come from the game info panel on duel.com, and the exact royal flush multiplier of 812.9719437907615 is taken from the config file captured from duel.com in the ProvablyFair.org audit repo. Above that bet size the casino scales the table down, which is not modelled here.

Return with the best play

Return is what comes back per unit wagered when every deal is held the best possible way. Loss per 1,000 is in units over 1,000 units wagered.

9/6 against 8/5 is 22.45 units per 1,000 wagered. The 9/6 machine keeps 4.56 units of every 1,000 played and the 8/5 machine keeps 27.02. That gap of 2.2455 points comes from two pay lines. With 9/6 play a full house or a flush lands on 2.25% of hands, and each pays one unit less on 8/5.

Learning the 8/5 strategy is worth very little. Playing an 8/5 machine with the 9/6 strategy returns 97.2912%, only 0.0072 points below the best 8/5 play. Picking the machine matters far more than the small strategy changes between the two tables.

The Duel table checks out. The casino states 99.90% for optimal play on its tier 0 table. The calculator gets 99.9000% for the same pays, with the strategy re-solved for that table. The higher straight flush and four of a kind pays do most of the work. The royal adds 12.97 units over 800.

Never keep a made hand over 4 to a royal. Holding a pair of kings instead of four cards to the royal gives up 1699.54% of the bet. Even a pat flush should be broken, at a cost of 1355.32% if you keep it.

Kickers are a habit, not a strategy. An ace kept next to a pair of jacks costs 12.03%. Standing on two pair instead of drawing one card costs 59.57%.

The paytable changes the price of a mistake. Breaking a pair of queens for 4 to a flush costs 34.51% on 9/6 and 52.64% on 8/5, because the flush pays less. A low pair against 4 to a flush goes the other way. The flush draw is right on both, but the gap shrinks from 38.91% to 20.78%.

Some arguments are close. Keeping 4 to an inside straight with two high cards instead of just the high cards costs 1.51%. Drawing five new cards beats a low inside straight draw by 1.89%.

Method and checks

Computation. The script in code/ goes through all 2,598,960 five card hands once and records, for every set of zero to five cards, how many final hands of each rank contain that set. For any deal and hold, inclusion and exclusion over the discarded cards then gives the exact number of ways to make each hand on the draw. Deals that differ only by swapping suits play the same, so 134,459 distinct deals are solved and weighted by how often they occur. All sums use whole numbers over a shared denominator, so nothing is rounded until the CSV is written. Run python code/build_data.py to rebuild every file. It needs Python 3 and nothing else, and takes about a minute with roughly 500 MB of memory.

Check against published tables. The returns match Wizard of Odds to every published digit, 0.99543904 for 9/6 and 0.97298434 for 8/5. The combination counts in hand-frequencies.csv match the Wizard of Odds counts exactly for every hand on both tables, for example 493,512,264 royal flushes out of 19,933,230,517,200 on 9/6.

Hold list check. The 16 line list is coded in code/strategy.py and scored over every deal. When two holds fall on the same line, the one with the higher exact value is used. The result, 99.4593%, matches the 99.46% Wizard of Odds gives for the list.

Limits. These are long-run averages for a single hand game with a fair shuffle. They say nothing about one session, and they are not advice to play.
